# Institutional and Manager Experience: Turn Accounts into Tests

These are attributed public accounts and proposed research responses. They are
not recreated portfolios or universal causal explanations. The source supports
the scoped account; the proposed diagnostic needs its own observations. Use the
relevant principles in [allocation](../knowledge/portfolio-risk.md),
[execution](../knowledge/execution-funding.md) and
[lifecycle](../knowledge/lifecycle-computation.md).

## Bridgewater: choose what to diversify before optimizing weights

Bridgewater's retrospective develops cash/beta/alpha separation, risk balancing
and growth/inflation-surprise coverage, including inflation-linked bonds. Its
account explains an allocation philosophy rather than disclosing a reproducible
proprietary portfolio. [The All Weather Story](https://www.bridgewater.com/research-and-insights/the-all-weather-story).

**Practice consequence:** build an economic exposure/stress map before asset ERC.
Check omitted inflation and real-rate sensitivities and common shocks. Compare
base allocation with any trend supplement separately; the risk-budget example
specifies its own modest public experiment. Environmental labels do not guarantee
that some asset always rises enough to offset every loss.

## Asness on August 2007: a temporary event can still kill a strategy

Cliff Asness's 2017 retrospective interprets the August 2007 quant losses as a
liquidity-driven unwind and emphasizes the danger of even temporary shocks.
This is a manager's interpretation, not a promise of future recovery.
[Asness's account](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Perspectives/The-August-of-our-Discontent-Once-More-Unto-the-Breach).

**Practice consequence:** compare forecast losses, crowded holdings, market depth,
costs and accessible financing. Stable forecasts with failed realization call for
capacity/funding changes. Reweighting similarly exposed experts does not remove
their shared liquidity dependence. Examine permanent relation change as a live
alternative rather than adopting recovery as an invariant.

## Institutional execution data: a factor factory needs a size-aware consumer

Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz study live institutional execution data and cost
variation across size, stocks, trade type, time and venues. Their sample is not a
universal fee table for another market or fund.
[Trading Costs](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/working-paper/trading-costs).

**Practice consequence:** retain turnover, liquidity, feasible order schedule and
a frozen size ladder beside forecast scores. Estimate costs for that consumer,
then confirm incremental net value. Neither a flat optimistic fee nor an unrelated
institution's pessimistic cost estimate can substitute for that bridge.

## LTCM: terminal relations and funding stress are different obligations

Greenspan's 1998 testimony describes shrinking opportunities, leverage, severe
losses and fire-sale concerns around LTCM.
[Federal Reserve testimony](https://www.federalreserve.gov/boarddocs/testimony/1998/19981001.htm).

**Practice consequence:** stress widening relationships, collateral and liquidity
together. Record cash deadlines and residual concentration; a plausible eventual
convergence cannot supply missing interim funds. This account does not identify
one universal failed signal or say every relative-value loss has the same cause.

## UK LDI, 2022: liability improvement can coexist with a cash crisis

The Bank of England reports that higher yields could improve defined-benefit
pension positions while leveraged LDI funds faced collateral calls, operational
cash delays and forced gilt selling.
[December 2022 report, Section 5](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2022/december-2022).

**Practice consequence:** stress accessible cash by deadline, haircuts, leverage
and sale feedback alongside economic solvency. Inflation-linked or nominal-duration
holdings still have rate/liquidity risk. A consolidated favorable balance sheet
cannot automatically meet a particular fund's immediate call.

## Metallgesellschaft: hedge horizon, basis and cash timing

Kuprianov's Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond study discusses Metallgesellschaft's
long-horizon commitments, derivative hedges and competing interpretations of its
failure. It also distinguishes operational, market, credit and legal risks.
[Derivatives Debacles](https://www.richmondfed.org/-/media/RichmondFedOrg/publications/research/economic_quarterly/1995/fall/pdf/kuprov.pdf).

**Practice consequence:** replay dated commitments, short-contract rolls, basis,
variation calls and financing. Compare rival explanations with their cash paths;
“hedged” does not specify survival or settle the controversy about the original
strategy. A toy forward equality omits these timing obligations.

## Amaranth: participation can change the relation being traded

The Senate's 2007 release attributes natural-gas price effects to concentrated
Amaranth trading. The CFTC's 2009 consent order settled attempted-manipulation
charges. These are distinct source findings, not evidence of a risk-free spread.
[Senate account](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/subcommittees/investigations/rep/investigations-subcommittee-releases-levin-coleman-report-on-excessive-speculation-in-the-natural-gas-market/),
[CFTC settlement](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/5692-09).

**Practice consequence:** bound position/depth, cross-venue concentration, exit
impact and participation feedback. Confirmation at a tiny size cannot certify
the same relation or unwind price at a large share of available liquidity.

## Knight Capital: faithful model code is only one operational layer

The SEC's 2013 account identifies deployment and market-access control failures
in Knight's August 2012 incident.
[SEC findings](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-222).

**Practice consequence:** test version identity, bounded order/exposure limits,
reconciliation, stop behavior and recovery for a separately authorized system.
An accurate offline predictor does not establish deployment readiness. These
checks belong to the actual implementation/operation and do not authorize live
order tests during a research request.

## FTX/Alameda: counterparty access belongs in the path model

The CFTC's 2024 judgment account describes customer-asset commingling and misuse.
[CFTC judgment account](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8938-24).

**Practice consequence:** limit custody exposure and stress inaccessible balances,
withdrawals and hedge proceeds independently of basis/funding arithmetic. A model
ledger can reconcile intended payments while the counterparty fails to provide
them. Product and legal facts require current authoritative terms for new use.
