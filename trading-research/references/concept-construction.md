# Concept Construction and Measurement

Use when a trader's term, observed pattern, or proposed alpha depends on an
unsettled explanation or relation. The aim is a concept that can make a different
research or trading decision, not a mandatory ontology, model family, or report
template. Preserve fixed user or project rules; do not manufacture a mechanism
story merely to use this route.

## Develop consequential relations and opposition

Begin with the practice or phenomenon and the research question or possible
decision it might affect. Identify the participants, constraints, available
actions, and temporal or group context needed for that inquiry. Treat a familiar
term as a provisional relation among these, not as a label to be fitted from a
chosen indicator. The possible decision role may emerge or change as cases,
relations, and observation limits become clearer.

When an observation admits materially different processes or one process has
opposing effects across states, build a serious counter-concept. Derive the
conditions and transition under which each account changes a research or
decision consequence. Ground rivals in participants, constraints, and time order;
do not manufacture a verbal negation, interchangeable story, or retrospective
label merely to populate a method.

Draw on other fields only by transferring a *relation or operation*, not a name:
map the actors or objects, interactions, any conserved or accumulating quantities,
time order, and intervention or observation available in both domains. State
which assumptions fail in the market setting and derive a consequence that the
incumbent account does not already predict. Use the transferred view as an
analogy or model proposal until evidence supports the market claim; do not turn
"contagion," "phase transition," or "game" into a fact by analogy.

Do not choose a preferred concept by narrative fluency. Prefer a construction
that handles the motivating and contrary cases where present, preserves the
distinctions needed by the inquiry, and yields a new consequence or refusal
boundary. If rival views change neither the current research move nor an
attainable action, do not expand the inquiry merely to settle their vocabulary.

## Derive mathematical structure before an estimator

Compare motivating, contrary, and boundary cases when the relation is unsettled:
what objects, order, accumulation, or group dependence persists, and where does
it change? Let that pattern propose an operation; revise the operation if a case
or attainable observation defeats it. These cases construct the concept, not a
claim about frequency or prospective coverage.

Ask what operation the concept must support: temporal precedence or duration;
stock versus flow and replenishment; directional influence among members of a
changing group; strategic response; state-dependent feedback; or a decision under
partially observed conditions. Choose the least structure that can express the
needed relations. Depending on the obstruction, it may be an interval relation,
partial order, dynamic graph, balance relation, event process, state-transition
system, game, or set of admissible latent states. These are candidate forms, not
an indicator or model menu to cycle through.

When more than one mathematical family can represent the needed relation,
construct materially different candidates and compare what each retains,
forgets, and predicts. Search across structural rather than merely numerical
alternatives: an order or graph may encode relational leadership where a scalar
ranking cannot; a coupled dynamic model may encode opposing feedback where a
static correlation cannot. Derive a discriminating consequence and data cost for
each live candidate; neither familiar indicators nor sophisticated notation win
by default. Do not impose a fixed number of candidates.

Expose the bridge: relevant objects and types, their relations and legal
operations, necessary constraints, any competing transition accounts, and a
research- or decision-bearing consequence. When a scalar, time slice, or fixed
threshold erases role, order, or context, retain the relation or partial order
first; thresholds or fitted scores may come later if the decision requires them.
A formal definition does not establish that its state exists in the market, can be
observed, predicts an outcome, or produces net alpha. Separate the proposed
market structure, the target quantity or relation (estimand), and the statistical
estimator used to learn it. More mathematical complexity is not itself progress.

For a proposed computational use, derive an effective route: encoding of
admissible inputs, a finite operation or controlled approximation, its output,
the relation it preserves, and the cost and latency relevant to its proposed use.
Trace a small admissible case from the proposed market relation through that
operation to the record fields and as-of time that would supply its inputs; use a
contrary case when it can reveal a lost distinction. If this bridge fails,
revise the affected source, assumption, representation, or role rather than
optimizing an estimator on substitute fields.
When that route fails, locate the break rather than dropping the concept:

- **Construction incomplete:** objects, relations, or legal operations are too
  weak or ambiguous to calculate the intended result. Extend or revise the
  mathematical construction, not its numerical threshold.
- **Migration obstructed:** the operation is defined but required inputs are not
  identifiable from available records, computation is too costly for its role,
  an approximation erases the key distinction, or its output cannot change the
  proposed research move or action. Seek a source or explicit assumption, a
  cheaper preserving representation, a weaker claim or different role, or a
  bounded stop.

Distinguish formal computability, computability from available observations,
timely computation, and practical decision effect. Failure to find an algorithm
does not prove noncomputability; a formal proof does not establish an executable
advantage. A concept that yields a new discriminating observation may advance
research without itself becoming a live indicator. Descriptive and deductive
tasks need not manufacture software.

For example, high group participation could reflect continuing diffusion or
new buyers supplying exits to earlier holders. A "climax" proposal must express
the timing and opposition between entry, exit, group propagation, and price
response if those distinctions determine its use. Entry and exit require
directional flows, propagation a time-indexed relation among group members, and
"climax risk" a proposed transition of their joint state rather than a
retrospective maximum. Counting high-turnover names alone cannot decide which
process dominates. This illustrates a construction, not a portable rule for
A-shares or any other market.

## Construct a decision-bearing representation

Treat a bar, event record, derived indicator, or composed state as an intentional
transformation of observations available at a decision time. From the proposed
relation, derive what an output must retain, then choose the input streams,
alignment, aggregation or other operation, and output grain. For example,
relative leadership needs a defined and potentially changing comparison group;
replenishment needs a relation between flow and a revocable stock; propagation
needs order across members and time. These are reasons to select operations, not
fixed indicators to append to every model. Trade events, quotes, depth updates,
and cancellations have different scopes; they do not form one mandatory ladder
from raw truth to progressively worse summaries.

Compare candidate representations by their proposed role, as-of availability,
preserved relation, information lost, computation and acquisition cost, and the
research move or action that their output can change. When sufficiency is
contested, try a pair of admissible histories with the same output but different
consequences under live accounts. If such a pair matters, repair the operator,
condition it on missing state, or seek the smallest accessible observation.
Failure to find a pair does not prove sufficiency; absent a known consequential
loss, a cheap coarse representation may be provisionally adequate. Do not
descend to finer data by default. An indicator can generate candidates,
estimate state, rank, time, constrain, or veto a decision without independently
identifying a unique mechanism or proving net alpha. Combining indicators is
justified by complementary relations and valid timing, not by feature count.

## Derive the observation map and data need

For each decisive relation, trace the latent object or event to an observable
record with its unit, grain, event time, availability time, market and population
coverage, direction or actor semantics, revision history, and source owner.
Distinguish the measured quantity from the target it may only proxy: executed
volume is a flow, displayed book depth is a revocable stock, and attention or
actor identity may require another source altogether. When identification is
disputed, ask whether different admissible market processes can produce the
same available observations yet change the inquiry or action. If so, identify
the smallest additional observation or assumption that separates them, or mark
the claim partially identified or untestable. No nonlinear learner recovers a
distinction that the admitted observation map has erased.

Source choice and concept formation constrain each other. Check historical
availability for the actual target period and what would have been known at the
decision time; a current snapshot does not recreate a past state. Failure of one
endpoint is not proof that no historical source exists; examine relevant archives
or alternatives only as far as that difference could change the claim. If the
needed source is absent, keep the supported descriptive or predictive claim
separate from the unverified mechanism, or revise only the affected concept and
decision role. Do not rename an available proxy as the missing construct.

## Construct the evaluation and discriminate

Where materially different accounts are live, derive signatures that distinguish
them, including time order and contrary cases. Select mathematics and statistics
for the current question: for example, a continuous state-dependent response
rather than arbitrary bins, a time-to-event comparison rather than one invented
horizon, or a relational test rather than a single-asset score. These are
conditional examples, not required sophistication. Address common causes,
feedback, selection, multiple exploration and dependence to the extent they
change the claim. A fitted association is not a causal pathway; a mechanism
account is not a prediction; neither alone establishes an attainable edge.

Construct the comparison for the question being asked. A measurement diagnostic
may ask whether two proposed states remain distinguishable; a decision test may
ask whether using the representation changes an attainable action relative to a
baseline. Define the opportunity set, information time, outcome and feasible
comparison for that role before choosing the statistic. Only a claim of
executable advantage calls for a post-cost decision comparison; failure of an
intermediate indicator to profit alone does not erase its possible role in a
conditional system, whose incremental use must be checked before claiming alpha.

Before automated search, check that the evaluator rewards the intended result
and cannot be satisfied by a changed label, easier population, later-available
input, or unfavorable results left unreported. If the evaluator is disputed,
work on its definition or limitation instead of treating a high score as a
market discovery. Preserve the consequential search path when later evidence
depends on how alternatives were selected; a polished final explanation cannot
substitute for that path.

If local tests keep returning results compatible with several accounts, do not
serially vary the window or threshold. Show the observational equivalence or
missing relation, then choose between a new authorized source, a weaker claim,
a different role, or stopping. Return the supported concept and any material
contrary case, the mathematical and observation bridges actually established,
the research or decision consequence, and the exact unresolved edge. This is a
semantic result, not an obligation to create a document or research ledger.
